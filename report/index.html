Who's actually serving your model?

What buyers can and can't see when they call an open-weight model through a third-party host

When you call GLM-5.3, DeepSeek V4 or Kimi K3 through a router, you usually aren't calling the lab. You're calling one of dozens of hosts running their own copy of the weights. This report shows what those hosts say about themselves, what independent teams measured, and what nobody can check yet.

01

Summary

  • Many listings don't state a precision. For 289 of 735 endpoints (39%), OpenRouter's API lists precision as unknown. 107 (15%) declare a 4-bit format.
  • Popular models come in many variants. GLM-5.3 has 37 endpoints: 16 with no stated precision and 8 declaring 4-bit. Output prices run from $1.19 to $8.80 per million tokens, and advertised context from 262K to 1.31M tokens.
  • Labels don't predict quality. Independent teams measured the same weights scoring 90% at one host and 75% at another, and a provider declaring fp4 outscoring one declaring fp8.
  • The checks that exist stay where they were made. They cover one platform, one lab's models, or one point in time. Nothing travels with the output.
02

How this was measured

We called GET https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/{model}/endpoints for every open-weight model OpenRouter lists from 11 author namespaces (DeepSeek, Moonshot, Z.ai, Qwen, OpenAI gpt-oss, Meta, Mistral, MiniMax, NVIDIA, Google Gemma, THUDM). The call is public and needs no key.

Every field in sections 3 and 4 is what the provider reports to OpenRouter. None of it was tested. Some endpoint tags appear twice in the listing (746 listings, 735 distinct), and the counts use distinct endpoints. The raw JSON ships with the report in the Assay repo.

A 4-bit label doesn't automatically mean a worse model. Some releases are trained to run at low precision, and section 5 shows labels failing to predict scores in both directions. That's why the next step is measurement, not labels.

06

What's missing

Each existing check stops somewhere:

  • OpenRouter grades hosts continuously, but only for traffic inside OpenRouter.
  • Moonshot, MiniMax and OpenAI publish verifiers, each for its own models. Moonshot's K3 table covers 7 vendors, while OpenRouter lists 17 providers for kimi-k3.
  • Artificial Analysis grades endpoints against official weights, but as dated snapshots, and it didn't list GLM-5.3 when we checked.
  • Marketplaces that sell from strangers say they don't measure this yet. Surplus: quantization "not measured". AntSeed: signed responses "prove who served which bytes", not the model.

None of these results travel with the output. Someone who receives a piece of AI-generated text can't check where it came from.

07

What comes next

Report v1 (by 11 Oct) grades every GLM-5.3 endpoint against Z.ai's own endpoint with an open harness. It checks tool-call correctness, parameter handling, context length and black-box identity, with 95% confidence intervals and raw logs. Any endpoint below the reference gets its logs and 7 days to respond before publication.

Assay receipts give every response a certificate of origin: the host's P-256 signature, an optional passkey co-signature from the requester, and salted hashes of the prompt and output anchored on Monad. Each receipt links to the host's latest grade from open verifiers.

Limits of this report

Sections 3 and 4 are self-reported fields captured at one moment, and listings change daily. Section 5 quotes other people's measurements, linked so you can check them, and we didn't re-run them. Nothing here claims a named provider serves a degraded model. It shows what a buyer can and can't see today.
